## post-exchange ruminations
exchange was fantastic. i spent too much money and didn’t study enough, but i loved every moment. when walking through the face detection immigration at singapore, the counter wished me “welcome home” for the last time a few weeks ago.

now, im on the flight from osaka to chennai, after my japan trip with friends. i haven’t seen family in a while, so im excited to meet them and travel a little with them as well. and tell them all about my exchange of course. 

i’ll start off with a few of my key learnings/takeaways from exchange at singapore. these will be more personal in nature. 

## yolo
1. yolo. push yourself just a little harder than you’ve been doing so far. the human body and mind are extremely capable, and you should get out of your comfort zone and try crazy new things. 

### yolo: rinjani
when charlie proposed that we climb mount rinjani, i remember we sat outside the utown erc (education resource center) and watched videos and read posts about rinjani. every post we read said it was the hardest thing they’d ever done. 

i was so so anxious to say yes, but i said fuck it and we booked the tour with a group of others (me, charlie, noah, rachel, dylan, john). 

### yolo: people are everything 
on day one, during the first hour of the hike, i felt exhausted. my head was throbbing a little, and i felt weak and slightly dehydrated. i told charlie i wouldn’t be able to make it to the top of even the crater rim, and that i was considering dipping at the first checkpoint (pos1). both charlie and dylan convinced me to keep going, and dylan stayed beside me the entire day to ensure i wasn’t falling behind. two germans (vincent and sophie) joined our group and were funny, genuine, and supportive people.

i made it to the crater rim and saw the most beautiful landscape i’ve ever seen in my life. it felt otherworldly. then, that night, the sky lit up with the milky way like i’ve never seen before.

the next day was the hardest. we went down to hot springs, knowing that there was a 2500ft ascent waiting after. the descent was dangerous and my feet were killing me by the end, since my boots kept chafing against the bottom of my toes and heels. but i made it, and we ascended back to the rim and had the best biscuits and dinner, ready for an early morning summit push. 

the summit push was difficult, but i forced myself to climb consistently. gravel kept shifting and two steps forward was one step back, for a grueling 3500ft ascent in just four hours. but at the summit, the joy of having climbed rinjani was immense, and the view was even better than the previous day. 

the way down was fun as we “skied” down the gravel slopes with our guide (who was a small 16yo (?) boy). what a legend. 

### yolo: bali 
i’ll keep this one shorter. john and i went together after our hike, snorkeled, and ate some fantastic food while chilling in the pool and swimming. we went to nusa lembongan the next morning, rented scooters, and swam in the ocean. 

then, we went to bali and met charlie and bene. we decided to rent scooters here as well, though traffic was quite bad (i don’t recommend you rent scooters if you’ve never driven in a place like bali before). i drove charlie around. we went surfing, went to the large garuda statue, and then to dinner. 

we met up with made simon the next morning and he took us around north bali. what a fantastic guy. i’m glad i reached out to him to organize a day trip, especially since he remembers me and my family from 10 years prior. 

### yolo: japan
onsen. just do it, no shame. freedom. you can’t let your mind constrain you.


## say yes to things.
this ties in with yolo, but say yes. dinner plans? say yes. crazy weekend adventure? say yes. walk across the entire country of singapore? yes. just do it. most likely, you’ll regret saying no.


## more technical takeaways/suggestions 
i arrived to NUS two weeks late, so i missed orientation and a bunch of events i could’ve gone to. here’s my take on what i’ve learnt, much of which id assume is also communicated during orientation events. 
1. NUS campus is huge. use the campus shuttle buses. the NUS NextBus app shows bus schedules, you should download it. 
2. if you can’t tolerate the heat, get an AC room. if you only manage to get a non-AC room, consider investing in a portable AC (you’ll need approval first). 
3. residents (eg students) in singapore get access to government ID called a SingPass (issued digitally, there’s an app). you can use this as ID anywhere. this is also your proof of residency status. 
4. you NEED a singaporean phone number to get a singpass. try to get a SIMBA sim card, since they allow part of the existing mobile data to be used internationally at no additional cost (good for traveling). 
5. if you need extra data when traveling, use saily. they’ve worked reliably for me in several countries and are reasonably affordable. 
6. payment systems in singapore are very difficult to north america. most locals use a method called PayNow, which is only accessible if you have a singaporean bank account. i don’t recommend opening a singaporean bank account. almost all places accept cash, so you should be carrying cash everywhere you go. only some places will accept card. 
7. on NUS campus, almost no food stalls accept card. use cash. in fact, i like using cash since im forced to be more responsible with my spending, and it feels more human.
8. if you want a debit card that pays directly in singaporean dollar (eg some card readers don’t accept foreign cards), use Wise. they offer good conversion rates.
9. Wise is good to have as a local debit card in singapore AND for traveling, since you can top money up in several currencies. it’s also great as a way to send money between friends (eg if you owe someone, send them money through Wise). 
10. transport within singapore is good. the singapore subway (SMRT) is extensive and accepts contactless payment methods (eg apple pay). you cannot eat or drink in the SMRT and the government is very strict about this.
11. always stand on the left on escalators in singapore. different countries in asia will have different customs for this. 
12. NUS courses are graded on a letter-grade basis, and the conversion from percentage to letter grade is dynamic (ie “graded on a curve”). passing grade is typically 50% but this can vary. it’s impressively hard to fail a course, but it’s also hard to do well. many courses allow an A4 two-sided cheatsheet for examinations, and the profs use this as excuse to make the exams even harder.
13. NUS courses care about attendance, and tutorials are often MORE important than lectures (eg tutorial participation, even for math/cs courses). the opposite is true in many other universities. 
14. consider taking non-bird courses during exchange. the pass/fail final grade can allow you to transfer credits from hard courses without worrying about the numeric grade. i personally took 4 core math/cs courses (algorithms, stochastic processes, stats, and real analysis). 
15. if you’re looking for coop during exchange, things will be hard. i had many interviews that were either extremely late at night, or very early in the morning. any (but especially technical) interviews at 4am can be killer, especially since waterloo’s cycle 1 interviews coincide with NUS midterm week. good luck balancing daytime midterms and midnight interviews. having said that, it seems as though employers like the fact that you’re on exchange, and it gives you more talking points + a meaningful excuse if you do perform poorly. 
16. pack minimally. you’ll likely buy a lot in singapore and in the places you travel to, and need the extra space to take things home afterwards. 
17. learn some basic singlish (singaporean english). for instance, ordering varieties of coffee are different in singlish than english (eg kopi-C for coffee with condensed milk). it might take a while for you to understand the cashier. 
18. don’t plan all your trips pre-exchange. you can find friends and groups to join while you’re there, and i highly recommend doing so. the best times to travel are pre-midterms and pre-finals and post-finals, since NUS gives breaks during these times.
